# Djassa Business Model

## Customer segments

### Primary customers

- Independent merchants and small retailers.
- Informal businesses that need repeat customers and better revenue visibility.
- Community groups that already operate tontines or savings circles.

### Financial partners

- Microfinance institutions and banks that want better distribution and alternative business data.
- Payment aggregators and mobile-money operators.
- Public or donor-backed guarantee programs.

## Value exchange

### What merchants receive

- A simple loyalty program without building their own software.
- Better visibility into repeat customers and transaction activity.
- A structured revenue history that may support a future financing application.
- Optional access to partner services, subject to consent and eligibility.

### What customers receive

- Clear loyalty benefits.
- A portable view of their own activity and rewards.
- Access to digital tontines or savings products through approved partners.
- Financial explanations that are understandable and timely.

### What partners receive

- A distribution channel into small businesses and communities.
- Consent-based, structured information that can improve assessment and service delivery.
- Lower operational friction for onboarding and recurring payments.

## Revenue model

Revenue should be layered over time. The first revenue stream must not depend on credit approval or on Djassa holding funds.

1. **Merchant SaaS subscription**
   - Monthly fee per outlet.
   - Tiers based on active customers, transaction volume, or features.
   - This is the primary MVP revenue stream.

2. **Partner referral fees**
   - A regulated lender or savings provider pays for qualified, consented referrals.
   - Fees must not create unfair pressure on users or determine access to basic service.

3. **Payment or tontine service fees**
   - A transparent fee for payment orchestration or digital tontine processing.
   - The flow of funds must use an approved provider and comply with applicable rules.

4. **Partner software or data services**
   - Reporting, reconciliation, or consented risk-data services for institutions.
   - Personal data must not be sold casually; aggregation, anonymization, purpose limitation, and consent must be defined before launch.

## Unit economics to measure

For each pilot merchant, track:

- Monthly recurring revenue.
- Acquisition cost and onboarding time.
- Active customers per outlet.
- Transactions recorded per active merchant.
- Retention after 30, 60, and 90 days.
- Messaging and payment costs per transaction.
- Support cost per merchant.
- Gross margin by plan.

For partner-finance experiments, track separately:

- Qualified referrals.
- Applications started and completed.
- Approval and repayment outcomes, where legally shareable.
- Referral revenue.
- User consent and withdrawal rates.

## Example pricing experiment

Pricing should be tested with merchants rather than treated as final. A simple first experiment could offer:

- **Starter:** one outlet, basic loyalty, low customer limit.
- **Growth:** higher activity limit, reporting, campaign tools.
- **Partner:** multiple outlets, exports, and partner-finance workflows.

Use local currency and publish whether messaging, payment, and partner fees are included. Avoid promising credit access as part of a paid plan.

## Commercial message

The strongest merchant message is:

> Every sale recorded with Djassa helps build a clearer history of the business and can make future partner financing easier to assess.

This must be presented as a possible benefit, not a guaranteed loan or a requirement for access to credit.

## Financial and regulatory boundaries

Djassa must not:

- Hold deposits or savings balances in its own account.
- Lend directly without the required authorization.
- Guarantee credit approval.
- Share identifiable transaction data without a defined purpose and valid consent.
- Charge hidden fees to customers.

Before launching payment, savings, scoring, or credit features, obtain legal and regulatory advice and confirm the role of the licensed partner.

## Pilot decision gate

Scale only when the pilot demonstrates:

- Consistent transaction capture by participating merchants.
- Repeat customer usage.
- Merchants willing to pay or renew.
- A measurable operational advantage for at least one financial or payment partner.
- No unresolved critical privacy, authorization, reconciliation, or regulatory issue.
